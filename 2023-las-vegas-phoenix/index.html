Amboy → Joshua Tree64 mi · 1 hr 6 min

Breakfast at Primm & Proper inside the Primm Valley Resort & Casino. The avocado toast, with poached egg, tomatoes and wild greens on country-grilled bread, is hard to beat.

No trip through the Mojave is complete without Roy’s Motel & Cafe in Amboy. The 1940s diner no longer serves hot food, but there’s Route 66 Root Beer and snacks to hold you until Joshua Tree. The pumps work, at some of the highest gas prices on Route 66.

At the gateway to Joshua Tree National Park, the Joshua Tree Saloon does food, beer on tap and live music.

Must do: Hike Hidden Valley, an easy one-mile loop among the rock walls, with succulents and wildflowers in late winter and spring.

Must see: Keys View, over 5,000 feet up, looks across the Coachella Valley to Palm Springs, Indio and the San Andreas Fault, and on a clear day to Signal Mountain in Mexico.

Day 4 · Fri, Dec 29

Sedona, AZ Globe, AZ

~263 mi

The road

Tortilla Flat & the Apache Trail

Desert mountains and Canyon Lake. Route 88 leads to Tortilla Flat; it was repaved in recent years, a huge improvement. Many turn back there, but the next 8 miles of good pavement are worth riding, and adventure bikes can carry on along the packed gravel toward Roosevelt for high canyon walls.

Day 5 · Sat, Dec 30

Globe, AZ Flagstaff, AZ

~272 mi

The road

US-60, Globe to Show Low

One of the best lesser-known rides in Arizona: down through the Salt River Canyon on a varied mix of turns, with hairpins near the river and good pull-offs for the views. There are few towns or gas stations, so fuel up when you can.

The road

AZ-89A through Oak Creek Canyon

South from Flagstaff’s forests into Sedona, turn after turn, with cliff overlooks and red-rock views. Many riders say southbound is the direction to ride it. Then the Beeline Highway, AZ-87, down to the Valley.
